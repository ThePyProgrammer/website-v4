This might be the coolest club I was involved in during my time at NUS High, which is unfortunate for it was also the one I was the least invested in. As part of our school's 15-year anniversary in 2020, the Galileo Programme was initiated to develop a full nanosatellite, from concept to planning to construction to takeoff. We had a lot of support from [Mr Ng Zhen Ning](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zhen-ning-ng-206676141/), the CEO of NuSpace and a nanosatellite expert.

I joined this team in 2021, looking to explore the crazy madness of engineering and prototype development. I was tasked to work on developing a telemetry, tracking and communications (TT&C) system for the nanosatellite, mostly housed on a TT&C module procured by my predecessors. We had to explore methods to compress the images from the camera procured to transmit across RF to the ground station, in a lossless manner. Initially, we had explored using JPEG 2000 (JP2) for their image processing format, and attempted to use the [JasPer Image Coding Toolkit](https://github.com/jasper-software/jasper).

However, with major support from senior Lin Yicheng, who had developed [a robust toolkit](https://github.com/ephemeridis/icer_compression) to use NASA's proposed [ICER algorithm](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-155/155J.pdf) for lossless compression of images, we integrated his system to our final prototype. Of course, I would say "we" but my junior, [Vincent Kwok](https://github.com/cryptoAlgorithm), did a bulk of the work, and Vincent and Yicheng deserve all the credit for their work on this satellite.

In case you're wondering, the satellite has indeed launched into space! [Check it out here!](https://satellitemap.space/sat/63211)
